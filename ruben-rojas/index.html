BME Student Spotlight

Ruben Rojas Betanzos

Engineering + Business + Impact: building a broader path through biomedical engineering.

Fifth-Year BME-MBA Student • Syracuse University • Fall 2026
Biomedical engineering has given Ruben Rojas Betanzos a technical lens for solving complex problems. His MBA experience has added another: how solutions are implemented, financed, communicated, and brought into the world. Together, those perspectives have shaped a Syracuse experience that spans clinical innovation, athletics, campus leadership, and preparation for a career in the life sciences.

Two Lenses on Engineering

From building the solution to understanding how it succeeds

For Ruben, the BME-MBA program has been less about choosing between engineering and business and more about learning to move between them. Engineering taught him to break down complex technical problems and develop solutions. Business added questions of economics, strategy, implementation, leadership, and the people a solution ultimately serves.

That combination has also opened a wider range of possible career paths, especially roles that sit at the intersection of technical knowledge, business strategy, and cross-functional leadership.

“Engineering taught me how to break down complex technical problems and build solutions, while the MBA has pushed me to think about implementation, economics, strategy, and the people a solution ultimately serves.”
Ruben Rojas Betanzos

Beyond Capstone

When a student project keeps moving

Ruben’s senior capstone team partnered with clinicians at SUNY Upstate Medical University and the Golisano Children’s Hospital McMahon Ryan Child Advocacy Center to develop a wearable imaging system for pediatric forensic examinations. The goal was to support accurate clinical documentation while creating a less intimidating, more practical experience for clinicians and young patients.

Capstone Project

Look, Click, Protect: A Forensic Wearable for Pediatric Examination

The team developed PFEx Glasses, a hands-free wearable imaging platform designed with input from clinical partners and potential users. The work earned a Social Impact Award at the 2026 BME Capstone Design final presentations.

Beyond the Classroom

Learning leadership through competing priorities

Ruben has also balanced academics with life as a student-athlete, involvement in Senate and student organizations, and other commitments across Syracuse University.

He does not describe balance as something perfectly solved. Instead, he focuses on being intentional with his time, staying organized, and choosing commitments where he believes he can contribute meaningfully. Athletics has reinforced discipline, teamwork, and accountability, while campus leadership has exposed him to people and perspectives from across the University.

“I try to be intentional with my time, stay organized, and focus on the commitments where I feel I can contribute something meaningful.”
Ruben Rojas Betanzos

Ruben’s “Secret Sauce”

Build broadly, stay connected, and keep the user in view

Learn across disciplines.

Technical depth matters, but understanding strategy, economics, implementation, and people can make an engineering solution much stronger.

Design with people.

Clinical partners and potential users can reveal practical and human factors that are difficult to see from an engineering perspective alone.

Be intentional with your time.

You do not need a perfect balance. Focus on commitments where you can contribute, learn, and support the people around you.

Looking Ahead

Toward pharma, life sciences, and cross-functional leadership

As he approaches graduation, Ruben is exploring opportunities in the pharmaceutical and life-sciences industries, particularly roles that bring together technical knowledge, business strategy, and leadership. It is a direction that reflects the broader path he has built at Syracuse: engineering as a foundation, but not a boundary.
